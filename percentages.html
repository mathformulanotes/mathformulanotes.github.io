Percentages

A percent is a number out of $100$. Once you can move among percents, decimals, and fractions, the usual word problems are just multiplication or a short rearrangement.

  • Percents, decimals, and fractions
  • Percentage of a number
  • Percent increase and decrease
  • Finding the original amount

Percents, decimals, and fractions

Divide a percent by $100$ to get a decimal, or write it as a fraction with denominator $100$ and reduce. Multiply a decimal by $100$ to get a percent.

$$p\% = \frac{p}{100}$$
Worked example

$15\% = \dfrac{15}{100} = \dfrac{3}{20} = 0.15$. Likewise, $0.08 = 8\%$ and $\dfrac{1}{4} = 25\%$.

Percentage of a number

The phrase “$p$ percent of $A$” means the product $\dfrac{p}{100}\cdot A$. A math solver can confirm the arithmetic when several percentages are chained together.

Worked example

What is $18\%$ of $250$? Compute $0.18 \times 250 = 45$. As a fraction, $\dfrac{18}{100}\times 250 = \dfrac{18 \times 5}{2} = 45$ as well.

Percent increase and decrease

The percent change from an old value $A$ to a new value $B$ is

$$\text{percent change} = \frac{B - A}{A}\times 100\%.$$

A positive result is an increase; a negative result is a decrease. The same formula is the usual starting point on the math solver arithmetic pages when a problem is written in words.

Worked example

A price rises from $80$ to $100$. The change is $\dfrac{100-80}{80}\times 100\% = \dfrac{20}{80}\times 100\% = 25\%$ increase. If the same $80$ price fell to $60$, the change would be $\dfrac{60-80}{80}\times 100\% = -25\%$, a $25\%$ decrease.

Finding the original amount

If a quantity is already a known percent of an unknown original $A$, divide to recover $A$. After a discount of $p\%$, the sale price is $\bigl(1-\dfrac{p}{100}\bigr)A$. After an increase of $p\%$, it is $\bigl(1+\dfrac{p}{100}\bigr)A$.

Worked example

A shirt costs $\$48$ after a $20\%$ discount. The sale price is $80\%$ of the original, so $0.8A = 48$ and $A = 48/0.8 = 60$. The original price was $\$60$.
