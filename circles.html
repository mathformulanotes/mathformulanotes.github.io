Circles

A circle is the set of points at a fixed distance, the radius, from a center. The formulas below cover length and area, the coordinate equation, and the portion of a circle cut out by a central angle.

  • Circumference and area
  • Equation of a circle
  • Arc length and sector area

Circumference and area

If the radius is $r$, the diameter is $2r$, the circumference (the distance around the circle) is $2\pi r$, and the area is $\pi r^2$. Leaving answers in terms of $\pi$ is exact; a decimal is only an approximation. A math solver can switch between radius, diameter, circumference, and area when one of those four is given.

$$C = 2\pi r,\qquad A = \pi r^2.$$
Worked example

A circle of radius $5$ cm has circumference $C=2\pi\cdot 5=10\pi$ cm and area $A=\pi\cdot 5^2=25\pi$ cm$^2$. If instead you are given a circumference of $14\pi$, then $2\pi r=14\pi$ so $r=7$, and the area is $49\pi$.

Equation of a circle

The circle with center $(h,k)$ and radius $r$ consists of the points $(x,y)$ that satisfy

$$(x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2.$$

If the equation is given expanded, complete the square in $x$ and in $y$ to recover the center and radius. The same distance idea appears throughout the math solver geometry pages.

Worked example

The equation $(x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=9$ describes the circle of radius $3$ centered at $(2,-1)$, because $y+1=y-(-1)$ and $r=\sqrt{9}=3$. The point $(2,2)$ lies on the circle: the vertical distance from $(2,-1)$ to $(2,2)$ is $3$, which equals the radius.

Arc length and sector area

A central angle of $\theta$ degrees cuts a fraction $\theta/360$ of the full circle. The corresponding arc length and sector area are therefore

$$L = \frac{\theta}{360}\cdot 2\pi r,\qquad A_{\text{sector}} = \frac{\theta}{360}\cdot \pi r^2.$$

If the angle is given in radians as $\theta$, the same formulas become $L=\theta r$ and $A_{\text{sector}}=\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.

Worked example

A $60^\circ$ central angle in a circle of radius $6$ gives arc length $L=\dfrac{60}{360}\cdot 2\pi\cdot 6 = \dfrac{1}{6}\cdot 12\pi = 2\pi$ and sector area $A=\dfrac{60}{360}\cdot\pi\cdot 36 = 6\pi$. In radians the same angle is $\pi/3$, and $\bigl(\pi/3\bigr)\cdot 6=2\pi$ agrees.
