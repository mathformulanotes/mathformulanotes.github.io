Inequalities

The same balancing moves that solve an equation also solve an inequality, with one extra rule: multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign. The solution is often a ray or an interval rather than a single number.

  • Linear inequalities
  • Compound inequalities
  • Absolute-value inequalities

Linear inequalities

Add or subtract the same quantity on both sides freely. If you multiply or divide both sides by a positive number, the sign stays as it is. If you multiply or divide by a negative number, flip $<$ to $>$ (and $\leq$ to $\geq$), or the other way around. A math solver can graph the resulting ray once you have the endpoint and the direction.

Worked example

Solve $3-2x > 7$.

  1. Subtract $3$: $-2x > 4$.
  2. Divide by $-2$ and reverse the sign: $x < -2$.
  3. Check a point in the region, say $x=-3$: $3-2(-3)=9>7$, true. A point outside, $x=0$: $3>7$, false.

Compound inequalities

A compact statement such as $a < x < b$ means $x$ is greater than $a$ and less than $b$. You may add the same number to all three parts, or multiply all three parts by a positive number. Multiplying by a negative number reverses both signs and swaps the order so the smaller number is again on the left.

Worked example

Solve $-1 \leq 2x+3 < 9$. Subtract $3$ from all three parts: $-4 \leq 2x < 6$. Divide by $2$: $-2 \leq x < 3$. In interval notation the solution is $[-2,3)$.

Absolute-value inequalities

For $k>0$,

$$\begin{aligned} |x| < k &\iff -k < x < k, \\ |x| > k &\iff x < -k \text{ or } x > k. \end{aligned}$$

The same rewriting works after a translation: replace $x$ by $x-h$. Absolute-value equations and inequalities are collected on the math solver absolute-value page if you want a second check.

Worked example

Solve $|x-3| < 5$ and $|x+1| \geq 4$.

  1. $|x-3|<5$ becomes $-5<x-3<5$. Add $3$: $-2<x<8$.
  2. $|x+1|\geq 4$ splits into $x+1\leq -4$ or $x+1\geq 4$, so $x\leq -5$ or $x\geq 3$.
