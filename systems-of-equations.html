Systems of equations

A system of two linear equations in two unknowns asks for the pair $(x,y)$ that sits on both lines at once. Substitution and elimination both work; the geometry tells you when there is one solution, none, or infinitely many.

  • Substitution
  • Elimination
  • No solution or infinitely many

Substitution

Solve one equation for one variable, substitute that expression into the other equation, then solve the resulting one-variable equation and back-substitute. A math solver is a convenient check once you have a candidate pair.

Worked example

Solve the system $x + y = 8$ and $2x - y = 1$.

  1. From the first equation, $y = 8 - x$.
  2. Substitute into the second: $2x - (8-x) = 1$, so $2x - 8 + x = 1$, hence $3x = 9$ and $x = 3$.
  3. Then $y = 8 - 3 = 5$. Check: $2(3)-5 = 1$, which matches the second equation. The solution is $(3,5)$.

Elimination

Multiply one or both equations by constants so that one variable has opposite coefficients, then add the equations to cancel that variable. The same idea extends to three equations in three unknowns, and it is close in spirit to the one-variable work on a math solver equation page.

Worked example

Solve $3x + 2y = 16$ and $x - 2y = 0$ by elimination.

  1. The $y$-coefficients are already opposites. Add: $(3x+2y)+(x-2y)=16+0$, so $4x=16$ and $x=4$.
  2. From $x-2y=0$, $4-2y=0$, so $y=2$.
  3. Check in the first equation: $3(4)+2(2)=12+4=16$. The solution is $(4,2)$.

No solution or infinitely many

After elimination or substitution, two things can go wrong in a useful way:

  • You reach a false statement such as $0=3$. The lines are parallel and distinct, so there is no solution.
  • You reach a true statement such as $0=0$. The two equations describe the same line, so there are infinitely many solutions.
Worked example

Consider $2x+3y=7$ and $4x+6y=10$. Doubling the first equation gives $4x+6y=14$, which contradicts $4x+6y=10$. The system has no solution. If the second equation had been $4x+6y=14$ instead, every point on the line $2x+3y=7$ would be a solution.
