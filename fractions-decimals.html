Fractions and decimals

Most fraction work comes down to a common denominator, cancelling common factors, and knowing when a decimal terminates or repeats. The examples below use only those ideas.

  • Adding and subtracting
  • Multiplying and dividing
  • Mixed numbers

Adding and subtracting fractions

To add or subtract $\dfrac{a}{b}$ and $\dfrac{c}{d}$, rewrite both fractions with a common denominator (the least common multiple of $b$ and $d$ is the most compact choice), then add or subtract the numerators and keep that denominator. Reduce the result at the end.

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad + bc}{bd}$$

A math solver is useful when the denominators are large and you want a check on the reduced form.

Worked example

Compute $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4}$.

  1. The least common denominator of $3$ and $4$ is $12$.
  2. $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{12}$.
  3. Add: $\dfrac{8}{12} + \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{11}{12}$, which is already in lowest terms.

Multiplying and dividing fractions

Multiply numerators together and denominators together, cancelling any common factor before you multiply if you like. Division is multiplication by the reciprocal:

$$\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd},\qquad \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c} = \frac{ad}{bc}$$
Worked example

Simplify $\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{10}{9}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{2}{5}$.

  1. $\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{10}{9} = \dfrac{3\cdot 10}{5\cdot 9}$. Cancel a factor of $5$: $\dfrac{3\cdot 2}{9} = \dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
  2. $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{5}{2} = \dfrac{15}{8}$.

Mixed numbers

A mixed number $n\dfrac{a}{b}$ means $n + \dfrac{a}{b}$. Convert it to an improper fraction before multiplying or dividing:

$$n\frac{a}{b} = \frac{nb + a}{b}$$
Worked example

$3\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3\cdot 2 + 1}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}$. Going the other way, $\dfrac{11}{4} = 2\dfrac{3}{4}$ because $11 = 2\cdot 4 + 3$.

Fractions and decimals

To write a fraction as a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator. The decimal terminates if the reduced denominator’s prime factors are only $2$ and $5$; otherwise it eventually repeats. To write a terminating decimal as a fraction, count the decimal places and use the matching power of ten, then reduce. These conversions sit naturally alongside the fraction work on the math solver pre-algebra pages.

Worked example

$0.375 = \dfrac{375}{1000}$. Divide numerator and denominator by $125$ to get $\dfrac{3}{8}$. In the other direction, $\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{4}{10} = 0.4$.
