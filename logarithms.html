Logarithms

A logarithm names the exponent you need. Once that definition is in place, the product, quotient, and power rules are just the exponent laws written in a different order, and change of base lets you evaluate any log on an ordinary calculator.

  • Definition
  • Product, quotient, and power rules
  • Change of base

Definition

For $a>0$, $a\neq 1$, and $x>0$,

$$\log_a x = y \iff a^y = x.$$

In particular, $\log_a a = 1$, $\log_a 1 = 0$, and $\log_a(a^k)=k$. The common logarithm is $\log_{10}$, and the natural logarithm is $\ln = \log_e$. A math solver is handy when the answer is not an obvious integer power.

Worked example

$\log_2 32 = 5$ because $2^5 = 32$. Likewise $\log_3 81 = 4$ because $3^4=81$, and $\log_5 \dfrac{1}{25} = -2$ because $5^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{25}$.

Product, quotient, and power rules

For positive $u$ and $v$ and a real number $k$,

$$\begin{aligned} \log_a(uv) &= \log_a u + \log_a v, \\ \log_a\!\left(\frac{u}{v}\right) &= \log_a u - \log_a v, \\ \log_a(u^k) &= k\log_a u. \end{aligned}$$

These identities are the usual first tools on the math solver precalculus pages when a log expression needs to be expanded or condensed.

Worked example

Expand $\log\left(\dfrac{x^2}{y}\right)$ for $x>0$ and $y>0$:

$$\log\left(\frac{x^2}{y}\right) = \log(x^2) - \log y = 2\log x - \log y.$$

In the other direction, $2\log_3 6 - \log_3 4 = \log_3(36/4) = \log_3 9 = 2$.

Change of base

Any logarithm can be rewritten in a new base $c>0$, $c\neq 1$:

$$\log_a x = \frac{\log_c x}{\log_c a} = \frac{\ln x}{\ln a} = \frac{\log x}{\log a}.$$
Worked example

To evaluate $\log_2 10$ on a calculator that has only $\ln$ or $\log_{10}$, compute $\dfrac{\ln 10}{\ln 2}$ or $\dfrac{\log 10}{\log 2}$. Both equal the same number, a little more than $3.32$, which makes sense because $2^3=8$ and $2^4=16$, so $\log_2 10$ lies between $3$ and $4$.
